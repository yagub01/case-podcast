## Course purpose

Based on the sessions covered, the course examines how venture capital and private equity funds are structured, how investors make and manage investments, and how the investor and entrepreneur sides of a deal fit together. It moves through a Foundations module, a PE module, and a VC module, using guests from many of the firms studied. Modules have included "Why VCPE?" and fund terms, private equity firms and deals, and VC careers, investment decisions, term sheets, and VC business models.

## Core frameworks and concepts
Course and assigned materials:
- **Fund waterfall:** How fund proceeds are split among limited partners and the general partner through return of capital, preferred return, catch-up, and carried interest. Used in Altoona and Whiskey Capital.
- **Fund economics:** Management fees, carried interest, hurdle rates, committed versus invested capital, and gross versus net returns.
- **GP catch-up:** The tier that lets the general partner receive its share after limited partners clear the hurdle. Whiskey Capital uses a 100% GP catch-up.
- **Endowment model:** Yale's approach built on diversification, inefficient markets, outside managers, and incentive alignment.
- **Fund size and GP economics:** Compares limited partner and general partner outcomes at different fund sizes. Used in Hollie Haynes / Luminate.
- **Operating value creation:** Operations, growth, and cost-cutting as sources of PE returns. Discussed in IFS / Kelso, the HBR reading on PE operating practices, and 3G.
- **MOIC and IRR:** Standard return measures used across fund and deal analysis.
- **Distressed lending and recovery waterfall:** Lender's view, Chapter 7 versus Chapter 11, involuntary bankruptcy, and who recovers what. Used in Oaktree.
- **Evergreen structure and crystallization:** 3G's fund structure, where 3G chooses the crystallization date.
- **Permanent capital:** Asset manager model built around long-duration capital and fee-related earnings. Used in KKR.
- **The Traits of Entrepreneurs:** Technical Note (HBS 9-823-099) separating traits that predict entry, survival, and the entrepreneur's own financial success.
- **Precision relational sourcing, vocation-led founders, and tailored partnerships:** Keith Bender's three pillars in the Vocation Capital case.
- **Liquidation preference, double dip, and as-converted waterfall:** Term sheet economics used in York Capital and Enovix.
- **Term sheet legal terms:** Vesting and buyback, board control, acceleration, protective provisions, representations and warranties, and related terms. Used in Tasnia Huque / Chombo.
- **Pro rata investing:** The right to invest enough in later rounds to maintain ownership. Used in Founder Collective.
- **Structural, financial, and empathic alignment:** Founder Collective's forms of alignment with founders.
- **Opportunity fund:** A separate fund for later rounds in a firm's best companies. Used in Founder Collective.

Outside sources used in prep chats (not confirmed as course material):
- **Venture Capital Method:** Scherlis and Sahlman, HBS 9-288-006 (1987). Stage-based venture discount rates applied to York Capital and Enovix.
- **The Founder's Dilemma:** Wasserman, HBR 2008. Applied to founder control in Tasnia Huque / Chombo.
